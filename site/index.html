what it keeps

[[how-it-remembers]]

Three layers,
all of them files.

Minne's memory is a folder — ~/Minne. There is no database of record, no embedding store, no Minne-private format. Open it in Obsidian, grep it, edit a page by hand, or delete the whole thing.

  1. sources/

    written by the app · never edited again

    It captures the window, not the screen.

    On every app switch, and every fifteen seconds you stay put, Minne walks the accessibility tree of your foreground window and appends what it reads to one file per app per hour. Card numbers, IBANs, SSNs and personnummer are replaced with ▮▮▮ before the write, so the unmasked text never reaches a disk. Password fields, password managers and incognito windows produce no file at all.

  2. wiki/

    written by the agent · cites everything

    Every half hour, it files what is new.

    The agent reads the snapshots it has not seen yet and decides who and what they were about: a page per person, per project, per recurring topic, plus a log for the day. Pages link to each other with [[wikilinks]] and every claim carries the snapshot it came from — so says who? always has an answer, and the answer is a file you can open.

  3. SCHEMA.md

    written by you · the rules it works to

    You own the contract.

    One human-owned file states what the agent may do: the page types, the frontmatter every page needs, how links resolve, what a citation looks like. Minne writes it once, when your memory is created, and never touches it again. Rewrite a rule and the next pass follows the new one.

[[the-minne-key]]

Tap right-Option.
Minne writes it for you.

In any text field, in any app. Minne reads what is on the screen, looks the people up in your memory, and drafts in your own voice — from style pages it learned by watching how you write. The field is not touched until you press Insert, and one keystroke puts it back.

  • empty field

    It infers the reply from the conversation already on your screen and what your memory knows about the people in it.

  • text selected

    It rewrites your selection — same point, your phrasing, tightened.

  • an instruction typed in

    Write “say yes but push to Thursday”, tap, and the instruction is replaced by the reply.

Right-Option is a bare modifier, so Minne watches for a tap and never a hold — ⌥-click, ⌥-drag and every Option shortcut on your Mac keep working untouched.

[[ask-your-memory]]

⌥Space,
then just ask.

What was I working on yesterday? Who did I mail about the Oslo trip? What did I promise Ingrid, and when? The answers come from the wiki, not from a guess — and Minne shows you what it searched on the way.

The chat window has the same tools the digest does, so you can ask it to fix a page, merge two people it confused, or write down something it never saw. It answers in markdown and cites the pages it read.

[[privacy]]

It stays on the machine.
That is the whole design.

Minne has no backend, because there is nothing for a backend to do. The only packets it sends are the calls to the model you signed in with.

  • No screenshots. Ever.

    Capture is text, read through the Accessibility API. There is no screen-recording code in the app to turn on later.

  • Masked on the way to disk.

    Card numbers (Luhn-checked), CVVs, IBANs, SSNs and Swedish personnummer become ▮▮▮ before the file is written. The snapshot counts its redactions so you can see it happened.

  • Blind spots you choose.

    Password fields and password managers are skipped out of the box, as are incognito windows. Add any app or domain, or pause capture for fifteen minutes, an hour, or until you say otherwise.

  • No account, no telemetry.

    Minne never asks who you are. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is counted, and there is no update ping.

  • One button erases it.

    Delete All Memory removes the wiki, every raw capture, the search index and your stored sign-in. Typed confirmation, no undo, no copy left behind.

[[your-model]]

Your subscription,
your quota,
your Mac.

Sign in with the Claude Pro/Max or ChatGPT Plus/Pro account you already pay for, paste an API key, or point Minne at Ollama and stay offline entirely. Chat and the half-hourly digest both run on whichever you pick — there is no Minne account and no Minne server in between.

Using a subscription from a third-party app is a gray area under provider terms: you are authenticating your own account, and the risk sits with it. API keys and local models are the always-safe paths, and Minne treats all three the same.
